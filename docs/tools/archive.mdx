Deleting a company, a person or an application does not destroy it. The record gets an
archive date, drops out of every list, board, picker, filter and count in the app, and
waits out a retention window — 30 days unless an admin changed it — before it is deleted
for good.

That means two different acts, and only one of them is final:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Reversible" icon="rotate-left">
    `archive_records` and the three `delete_*` tools. Everything they touch is still there
    and comes back whole with `restore_records`.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Final" icon="triangle-exclamation">
    `delete_archived` and `empty_archive`. Nothing is behind these — no second bin, no
    undo. Read the archive back to the person before calling either.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Neither final tool can reach a live record: both only ever match rows that are already in
the archive. Nothing in this app can be destroyed in one step.

<Note>
  Archiving a company takes every application still pointing at it, and restoring the
  company brings back exactly those — not one the person binned separately beforehand,
  which stays where they put it. The people who represent a company are never archived
  with it.
</Note>

Roles, highlights, notes, resumes, tasks, tags and saved views are not archived. Deleting
one of those is immediate and final, and their own tool descriptions say so.

### `list_archive`

_What is in the archive_

Everything this person deleted and can still get back. Deleting a company, a person or an application puts it in the archive, deleted for good a set number of days later — this tool reports the figure in force. Reach for it when they ask where something went, deleted something by mistake, or want to know what is about to disappear. One row per item: kind, id, name, a subtitle, when it was archived, and purgeAt — when it goes for good, or null when this instance keeps things forever. Each row says what would come back with it ('restoring Stripe brings 3 applications back'), and flags a company whose name a live company has since taken, the one thing that makes a restore fail. Pass kind and ids to restore_records, or to delete_archived to finish the job now. Read-only: saves nothing, purges nothing.

| Argument | Type | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `kind` | enum | Only this kind: company \| contact \| application  `company` · `contact` · `application` |
| `search` | string | Match the name, or a role title and its company |
| `limit` | number | How many of each kind at most. Default 200. |

### `archive_records`

_Delete, reversibly_

Delete records the reversible way — THE tool whenever somebody says to delete or remove a company, a person or an application, one id or many, one kind per call. They leave every list, board, filter and count and land in the archive, where restore_records brings them back for a set number of days (30 by default) before they go for good. Three things it is not: a job that ended is move_application_stage to LOST with a reason, because archiving takes it out of the funnel's record; a duplicate employer is merge_companies; and taking somebody off one application is update_contact with an empty applicationId. Archiving a company takes every application still pointing at it, timelines and tasks included, and restores them together. The people at that company are NOT archived — they just lose this company. Ids not theirs, or already archived, are skipped. Nothing here is permanent; delete_archived is.

**Overwrites or deletes.**

| Argument | Type | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `kind` | enum | **required** — company \| contact \| application  `company` · `contact` · `application` |
| `ids` | string[] | **required** — Ids of that kind |

### `restore_records`

_Bring archived records back_

Take records out of the archive and put them back in the app. Call list_archive first for the kind and ids. Restoring a company also restores the applications archived WITH it — not one the person archived separately beforehand. Restoring an application whose company is archived brings the company back too. Ids not in the archive are skipped, so restoring twice is harmless. The one real failure is a name: restoring 'Stripe' while a live 'Stripe' exists is refused for that company alone and reported in `skipped` with the reason — everything else still comes back, and preview_company_merge then merge_companies fold the two together. Returns what was restored, what came back alongside it, and what was skipped and why.

| Argument | Type | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `kind` | enum | **required** — company \| contact \| application  `company` · `contact` · `application` |
| `ids` | string[] | **required** — Ids of that kind, from list_archive |

### `delete_archived`

_Destroy archived records now_

Destroy archived records now instead of waiting for the retention window. IRREVERSIBLE: no second bin, no undo, no copy. It reaches only records ALREADY in the archive, so nothing can be destroyed in a single step. Destroying a company destroys every application archived with it, timelines and tasks included; a company with a LIVE application is refused. Call list_archive first, tell the person exactly what will go and how many, and get a plain yes before calling this. Usually there is nothing to do here — the archive clears itself — so this is only for something somebody wants gone now.

**Overwrites or deletes.**

| Argument | Type | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `kind` | enum | **required** — company \| contact \| application  `company` · `contact` · `application` |
| `ids` | string[] | **required** — Ids of that kind, from list_archive |

### `empty_archive`

_Empty the archive_

Empty the archive completely, or one kind of it. Everything in it is destroyed immediately and none of it comes back — the most destructive tool on this server, able to take years of applications, timelines and people in one call. Never reach for it because somebody said 'clean up', 'tidy my pipeline' or 'get rid of the old stuff'. It REFUSES unless expectCount matches the archive's count right now: call list_archive, tell the person how many things are about to go and what they are, get a plain yes, and pass back that count; if anything changed in between, the call fails. Use delete_archived for specific things. Returns how many of each kind were destroyed — a company takes the applications archived with it, so this can exceed the rows they saw.

**Overwrites or deletes.**

| Argument | Type | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `expectCount` | number | **required** — How many items list_archive just reported. The call fails if it moved. |
| `kind` | enum | Only this kind. Omit to empty the whole archive.  `company` · `contact` · `application` |
